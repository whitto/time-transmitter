The full suite and browser checks subsequently passed after publication. **The ESP32 compile is omitted at the user's request.** No physical ESP32/watch is attached.

## Problem and authorized scope

The prior missed-boundary counter measured forward wall-clock gaps even while RF was idle or after an intentional pause. Initial NTP acquisition can replace boot-relative seconds with UTC; the gap then appears as many missed boundaries. Current/worst task-arrival delay also accepted unqualified wall-clock samples. Overview's System card maps any nonzero cumulative missed count to **Check timing**, so this could remain visible after a harmless clock change or pause without evidence of an ongoing RF timing fault.

R4 must distinguish qualified continuous active RF progress from startup, idle/BLE, pauses, fault recovery and clock changes. Its counters must remain in fixed RAM, compare paired monotonic/wall samples and retain genuine task gaps. Encoder/envelope output, LF schedules, NTP admission, BLE protocol/ownership and pause semantics remain unchanged. The top-of-sketch release history and R4 metadata are maintained by the release integrator.

## Correction and embedded review

- A `radioTask`-owned `RadioBoundaryDiagnostics` object tracks adjacent wall/64-bit monotonic samples. Only continuous active RF with a consistent relationship between those samples contributes missed boundaries or current/worst task-arrival delay. First activation, idle/BLE, deliberate pause, fault and timer-unavailable paths reset the baseline/current delay while retaining genuine cumulative history.
- A diagnostic-only 32-bit atomic generation brackets every existing `settimeofday` path. Odd/inconsistent samples or a changed generation reset the baseline, excluding accepted corrections even below a millisecond. Both successful and failed SNTP commits complete the bracket; rejected replies do not advance it. The existing admission, commit and rejection decisions are unchanged.
- A wall/monotonic cross-check also excludes unmarked forward/backward discontinuities. Arithmetic validates the sample before conversion, uses 64-bit elapsed time and saturates lifetime counters rather than wrapping. Clock-sequence rollover, microsecond/`millis()` boundaries, midnight and 20 years of modeled uptime are covered.
- The helper occupies **40 bytes in the native fixture**, plus one 32-bit global atomic generation. It owns no heap, queue, callback, task or timer. Added work is fixed arithmetic/atomic access, without another mutex or I/O. The helper observation and snapshot publication run **after the existing RF envelope and buzzer writes** in each wake. Real ESP32 instruction cost and output-edge timing remain unmeasured.
- All new state stays in RAM. There are no configuration/history/crash-storage changes or additional flash writes. RF/BLE ownership and API diagnostic field names are retained. Independent byte comparisons confirm unchanged bodies for `ampchange`, `applyCurrentSchedule`, `radioProcessPauseCommand`, `getlocaltime`, `starttimer`, `stoptimer`, and all six LF encoder functions.

The current/worst figures remain **task-arrival diagnostics**, rather than calibrated antenna/output-edge measurements. A genuine missed-boundary count remains cumulative since boot; the System card can therefore continue to show Check timing after a real recorded gap, even when present operation is healthy.
